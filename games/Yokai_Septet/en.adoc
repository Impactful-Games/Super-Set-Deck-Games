A long time ago in the land of the rising sun, spirits and demons known as Yokai were harming human lives.
Yokai did not directly attack people, but they caused supernatural disasters: Yokai made farmlands burn, rivers flood, thunder roar, and diseases spread.

One day, a chieftain of the nearby village visited you, an Onmyoji, a practitioner of Japanese esoteric cosmology.
Through your knowledge of astronomy and occultism, you discovered the only way to help save the village.
It is simple: the more Yokai you manage to capture, the smaller the menace caused by these demons.
After a few months in the village, your efforts are successful: the Yokai menace is completely eradicated from that village!

Then, word reaches you of the havoc being wreaked by the Yokai on your own village.
In your absence, the village chieftain has consulted another Onmyoji to help save your village.
This is an affront, because this is your village and the people under attack are your people.
It is your job to save the day!

Do not let another Onmyoji save the day and go down in history as the greatest there ever was, the Savior of your village in its time of need.


== Game overview

In Yokai Septet, your goal is to capture high-scoring Boss Yokai (the K cards) by strategically playing cards from your hand.
Each turn, the players play a card from their hand following the rules of “trick-taking games”.
(If you don’t know what this means, don’t worry: it is explained in more detail later.)

After everyone has played a card, the player who played the strongest card takes all the cards played this turn.
New turns follow until one of the end-of-round conditions is met.
At the end of the round, the winner of that round is determined.

In a 4-player game, the game is played as a team battle of two players versus two players, whereas in a 3-player game, all players compete individually.
The winner of the round is the team that captured four or more Boss Yokai in a 4-player game, or the player who captured three or more Boss Yokai in a 3-player game.
However, be careful not to win too many tricks, because you lose the round if you win 7 tricks before collecting the required number of Boss Yokai!

The winner of the round scores points based on the Boss Yokai they captured.
New rounds are played until the end-of-game condition is met: 2 points for a basic game, or 7 points for a traditional or an advanced game.

[#round-setup]
== Round setup

Before starting each round:

* Shuffle all 49 cards face down, and deal *12 cards face down* to each player.
Place the *1 remaining card face up* at the center of the table, visible to all players.
This face-up card is called the *Trump Suit card*.
* All players choose *3 cards* and *pass them* to their teammate *at the same time*.

.3 players
[NOTE]
====
Deal 16 cards face down to each player.
Each player then passes 3 cards to the player on their left.
====

Now determine the lead player:

* *If this is the first round of the game:* +
the lead player is *the player who was dealt ★A* (that player must reveal it).
If no player was dealt ★A, then the lead player is the player who was dealt ♠13.
+
NOTE: The lead player does not need to start the game by playing the revealed card.
They may put it back into their hand and use it later in the round.
* *For all subsequent rounds:* +
the lead player is *the player who won the last trick* of the previous round.

The round is now ready to begin!


== Gameplay rules

Each round is divided into multiple turns called “*tricks*”, in which each player plays a card from their hand.

* Starting with the *lead player* and going *clockwise*, each player plays 1 card face up from their hand.
* The lead player may play any card from their hand.
The *other players must play a card of the same suit as the lead player’s card*.
* If you *do not have a card of the same suit* as the lead player’s card in your hand, then you may play *any card from your hand*.

After each player has played a card, the winner of the trick is determined.


=== Winner of a trick

The winner of a trick is determined in the following order:

. If *★A* was played, the player who played it is the winner.
. If cards of the *same suit as the Trump Suit card* were played, then the player who played the highest card among them is the winner.
. Otherwise, the player who played the *highest card of the same suit as the lead player’s card* is the winner.

If you are the winner of a trick, do each of the following in order:

. Take *all the cards* played in this trick (four cards in a 4-player game, three cards in a 3-player game).
If you took any Boss Yokai (K cards, of value 7), keep those cards *face up in front of you*.
Keep the rest of the cards face down in front of you, making a separate stack for each trick you win, so that everyone clearly sees how many tricks you have won in this round.
. Check whether one of the <<end-of-round,end-of-round conditions>> is met.
. If the round did not end, continue with the next trick.
*You are the lead player for the next trick.*

.Examples
====
Here are some examples showing who wins each trick.
The trump suit is ⎈ for this round.

*Trick 1*:

* Alice (lead): ★2.
* Barbara: ★5.
* Charlie: ★3.
* David: ♣8.

Barbara wins this trick, with the highest card of the lead player’s suit (★).
David does not win even though David’s card is the highest, because it is not of the lead player’s suit.

*Trick 2*:

* Barbara (lead): ♠K.
* Charlie: ♠13.
* David: ⎈8.
* Alice: ⎈5.

David wins this trick, with the highest card of the trump suit (⎈).

*Trick 3*:

* David (lead): ⎈K.
* Alice: ★A.
* Barbara: ⎈6.
* Charlie: ♦4.

Alice wins this trick, with ★A, which beats every other card in the game.
Although ★A is a special card, remember that you still need to follow the lead player’s suit.
Alice could play this card only because Alice did not have any ⎈ card in hand.

*Trick 4*:

* Alice (lead): ♠9.
* Barbara: ♥6.
* Charlie: ♣6.
* David: ✿4.

Alice wins this trick, since no one else followed suit: Alice’s card is the highest card of the lead player’s suit.
====


[#end-of-round]
== End of a round

The round is over as soon as *any* of the three conditions below is met:

. a team has won a total of *four or more* Boss Yokai (K cards, of value 7) between its two members;
. a team has won a total of *seven tricks* between its two members;
. the players have no cards left in their hand.

If none of the conditions above is met, continue with the next trick.


=== Winner of the round

Determine the winner of the round *in the following order*:

. If a team has won a total of *four or more* Boss Yokai, *that team is the winner*.
. Otherwise, if a team has won a total of *seven tricks*, *the other team is the winner*.
The winning team takes *all the Boss Yokai left in everyone’s hands*, and adds them to the stack of Boss Yokai they won during the round.
. If neither of the above happened, the team whose player won the *last trick* is the winner. +
*That team wins the Trump Suit card* (which must be a Boss Yokai: this only matters with the traditional scoring).

.3 players
[NOTE]
====
The winner of the round changes as follows:

. If a player has won *three or more* Boss Yokai, that player is the winner.
. If a player has won *seven tricks*, the other two players are the winners.
. Otherwise, the player who won *the last trick* is the winner. +
*That player wins the Trump Suit card* (which must be a Boss Yokai: this only matters with the traditional scoring).
====


== Score calculation

*Only the winning team of the round scores points.*

The winning team scores points according to the scoring rules below:

* play the *basic scoring rules* if this is your first time playing this game, or if players are new to “trick-taking games”;
* play the *traditional scoring rules* if you understand “trick-taking games” but want a simple way to score points;
* play the *advanced scoring rules* if all players are experienced with Yokai Septet.


=== Basic scoring rules

*The winning team scores 1 point.*

The game is over when a team has *2 points*.
That team is the winner.

Otherwise, go back to the <<round-setup>> to start a new round.

.3 players
[NOTE]
====
*Only the winners of the round score points.*

When playing with 3 players, there may be 2 winners in a round.
Who scores this round depends on how the round ended:

* if the round ended because a player won three or more Boss Yokai, *that player scores 1 point*;
* if the round ended because a player won seven tricks, *the other two players each score 1 point*.

The game is over when a player has 2 points.
That player is the winner.

If two players are tied with 2 points, then the last round ended with one player taking seven tricks.
The player to the left of the player who won seven tricks wins the game.

Otherwise, go back to the <<round-setup>> to start a new round.
====

=== Advanced scoring rules

To determine your score, look at all the *Boss Yokai* you won in this round.

Each Boss Yokai scores from 0 to 2 points:

[%autowidth]
|===
| ★ | ⎈ | ✿ | ♦ | ♣ | ♥ | ♠
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2
|===

The winner scores the points of each Boss Yokai they won during the round, *except for the Boss Yokai of the trump suit*.
You score 0 points for the Boss Yokai of the trump suit.

The game is over when a team has 7 or more points.
That team is the winner.

Otherwise, go back to the <<round-setup>> to start a new round.

.Advanced scoring
====
Alice and Charlie are in a team, and Barbara and David are in the other team.

*Round 1*:

Alice and Charlie won two Boss Yokai each, so the round is over, and they are the winners.

The Boss Yokai they won are ★K, ♦K, ♥K and ♠K.
That makes a total of five points, but ♥ is the trump suit, so they score 0 points for ♥K.
Therefore, the team scores a total of 3 points this round.

*Round 2*:

Alice and Charlie have won 3 tricks each.
Charlie has just won a 4th trick, so the team has now won a total of 7 tricks.

The round is over, and Barbara and David’s team is the winner.

Barbara and David add all the Boss Yokai left in everyone’s hands to the stack of Boss Yokai they won during the round.
They won ♣K, ⎈K, ♦K, ♥K and ✿K.
That makes a total of five points, but ♦ is the trump suit, so they score 4 points this round.

== Seven Suitors: a 4-player variant

This is a 4-player variant using two sets of K cards.
We recommend playing this variant only after everyone has played and understood the rules of the base game.


=== Game overview

The goal of this game is to be the first team to capture and seal the Boss Yokai of all seven suits.
To seal a suit, your team must capture both Boss Yokai (K cards) of that suit during a round (not necessarily in a single trick).


=== Before the game

Just like in the base game, split up into two teams of two players each.
The team members sit across from each other, so that each player’s opponents are on their left and right.

One team takes the 7 J cards, and the other team takes the 7 Q cards.
These cards are the “capture” cards: they are placed face up to the side of the table.

*Use two sets of K cards*.
You play with a deck of 56 cards.

The 0 cards are only used to show the trump suit, and are not shuffled with the game cards.


=== Round setup

Place the seven 0 cards in a face-down pile in the middle of the table.

Shuffle the deck of 56 cards.
Deal a hand of 14 cards face down to each player.
There are no cards left over.

Each player looks at their hand, and passes one card face down to each other player.
That is, each player passes 3 cards.

The player who was dealt ★A reveals it, and becomes the first lead player.


=== Gameplay rules

The lead player plays first.
They choose any card from their hand and play it face up in front of them.

*The suit of the first card played by the lead player in each round determines the trump suit for the rest of the round.*
Find the corresponding 0 card in the pile of 0 cards, and place it face up on top of the pile.

Every other player plays a card exactly as in the base game.
That is, they must play a card of the same suit as the lead player’s card, and may only play a card of another suit if they have no card of that suit.


=== Taking a trick

The trick is taken exactly as in the base game, except when both Ks of a suit are played and would win the trick.
In this case, the first K played wins the trick.

If you take the trick, take all the cards played in this trick.
If you took any Boss Yokai (K cards), keep them face up in front of you.
If your team has now taken both Ks of a suit this round, seal that suit for your team by turning your team’s capture card of that suit face down.

Next, the player who took the trick becomes the lead player of the next trick.
Keep playing tricks until all 14 cards have been played.


=== End of a round

The round is over once all the cards in your hand have been played, that is, after 14 tricks.

Play more rounds until a team has sealed all seven suits.
This generally takes several rounds.


=== End of the game

The game is over as soon as a team seals all seven suits, even in the middle of a round.
That team claims victory!


=== Shorter variant

Instead of having each team seal all seven suits, in this variant, each suit is sealed by the first team to capture both Ks of that suit in a round.
The first team to seal four of the seven suits wins the game.

Do not use the J cards.
Place the 7 Q cards face up to the side of the table.
When a team seals a suit, they take the corresponding Q card for themselves.
