= Okey Dokey
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 40 Number cards: 1-8 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ✿.
* 10 Reset cards: 2 K in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ✿.
* 3 Phoenixes.
****

All of you have gathered to organize a spectacular music festival.
Vocals, guitars, drums, pianos and bass from all over the land come together to play their part in this special event.
Many bands have gathered, and each performer has their part to play in creating a successful music festival!


== Object of the game

Okey Dokey is a cooperative game: the players are not competing, but striving together for the common goal of successfully staging the festival.
You play cards one by one, in accordance with the rules, to a 5 by 10 grid to create a colourful music festival.


== Goal of the game in detail

The goal of the game is to play all 50 Number and Reset (K) cards to a grid of 5 rows by 10 columns.

[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^,^,^,^,^,^,^,^,^,^"]
|===
h| ♠ | 2 | K | 1 | 3 | 7 | 8 | K | 4 | 5 | 6
h| ♥ | K | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | K | 1
h| ♣ | 1 | 2 | 5 | K | 3 | 4 | 6 | 7 | 8 | K
h| ♦ | 2 | 6 | K | 7 | 8 | K | 1 | 3 | 4 | 5
h| ✿ | 1 | 3 | 4 | 6 | K | 2 | 5 | K | 7 | 8
|===


== Setup

1. First, determine the difficulty level of the game.
The difficulty level determines the number of Phoenixes used in the game.
+
[%autowidth]
|===
| Difficulty level | Number of Phoenixes

| Amateur | 3
| Standard | 2
| Professional | 1
| Maestro | 0
|===

2. Shuffle the corresponding number of Phoenixes and all the Number cards well into a face-down deck.
Return the unused Phoenixes to the box: they are not used in the game.
Then, deal the Number cards (with the Phoenixes mixed in, but not the Reset cards) according to the number of players.
The table below gives the number of cards of each player:
+
[%autowidth]
|===
| Player count | Starting hand size

| 1 player | 10 cards
| 2 players | 7 cards
| 3 players | 6 cards
| 4 players | 5 cards
| 5 players | 4 cards
|===
+
Place the remaining cards in a Number card deck, within reach of all players.

3. Set up the Reset cards by placing 5 piles of 2 K each face up, in 5 rows:
+
[%autowidth]
|===
| ♠K
| ♥K
| ♣K
| ♦K
| ✿K
|===

4. Decide together on a start player.
Look at your own hands, and decide who should begin.
When the game begins, play proceeds in turns clockwise from the start player.
You are now ready to begin!


== Flow of the game

The game proceeds in turns until all 50 cards are placed in the grid.
The turns proceed clockwise from the start player.

CAUTION: *Exception*: In a 5-player game, when a player places the fifth card of a column, they immediately take another turn.

On your turn, carry out 1 of the following 2 actions:

a. Play a Number card.
b. Place a Reset card.

If, during your turn, you cannot carry out an action (neither (a) nor (b)), the game ends immediately and you have all lost the game.


=== a. Play a Number card

Play a Number card to the grid.
You can also play a Phoenix in place of a Number card.
You must obey the card placement rules below.
After playing a Number card, draw a card from the Number card deck to your hand, if any is left.


=== b. Place a Reset card

Choose a suit, and place a Reset (K) card of the pile of that row into the grid.
You cannot take this action if there are no Reset cards left of that suit.
You must obey the Reset placement rules below.
Then, if there is at least one card left in the deck, choose 0 to 2 cards from your hand, discard them face down to the bottom of the deck, and draw as many cards as you discarded from the top of the deck.


=== Drawing cards

Once the deck runs out, you can no longer draw cards.

In addition, when you place a Phoenix, you may discard a Number card of the suit of the row where you placed the Phoenix.
Place the discarded card under the Phoenix you just placed.
You may only discard one card per Phoenix.

If you run out of cards in your hand, skip your turn for the rest of the game.


=== Card placement rules

==== Column placement rules

You must place cards in the current column.
You can only start a new column when the current one is complete.
(For example, you can place a card in column 2 only after placing 5 cards in column 1.)

* *OK example*: The column has 5 cards, so the ♥4 *can* be placed.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^"]
|===
| ♠1 | _
| ♥3 | <- ♥4
| ♣2 | _
| ♦K | _
| ✿4 | _
|===

* *Error example*: The column only has 3 cards, so the ♥4 *cannot* be placed.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^"]
|===
| _  | _
| ♥3 | <- ♥4
| ♣2 | _
| ♦K | _
| _  | _
|===


==== Suit placement rules

All the Number cards of a row must be of the same suit.
In addition, there is exactly one row for each suit.
(For example, even if a Reset card is placed in the first column of the first row, all the subsequent cards of this row must still be ♠.)

* *OK example*: Same suit, so the ♥4 *can* be placed.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^"]
|===
| ♠2 | _
| ♥3 | <- ♥4
| ♣K | _
| ♦1 | _
| ✿4 | _
|===

* *Error example*: Different suit, so the ♥4 *cannot* be placed.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^"]
|===
| ♠2 | <- ♥4
| ♥3 | _
| ♣K | _
| ♦1 | _
| ✿4 | _
|===


==== Number placement rules

The number of the card played must be equal to or greater than the number of the card to its left.
You may play a Reset (K) card regardless of the number of the previous card.
Placing a Reset card resets the number to zero (that is, any Number card may be played after it).

* *Example*:
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,^,<"]
|===
| ... | ♠6 | <- Here, a ♠7, a ♠8, a Phoenix or a ♠K may be played.
| ... | ♥K | <- Here, any ♥ Number card, a Phoenix or a ♥K may be played.
|===


==== Reset placement rules

Every column of 5 cards must hold exactly 4 Number cards and 1 Reset (K) card.
At the end of the game, there must be exactly two Reset cards per row.

* *OK example*: There is exactly 1 Reset (K) card in this column.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^"]
|===
| ♠2
| ♥3
| ♣4
| ♦1
| ✿K
|===

* *Error example*: There are 2 or more Reset cards in this column.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^"]
|===
| ♠3
| ♥7
| ♣K
| ♦K
| ✿2
|===

* *Error example*: There are no Reset cards in this column.
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^"]
|===
| ♠3
| ♥4
| ♣8
| ♦1
| ✿2
|===


==== Phoenix placement rules

You may place a Phoenix instead of any Number card.
When a Phoenix is played, its number is the same as the number of the card to its left.
If the card to its left is a Reset (K) card, the number of the Phoenix is zero.

* *Example*:
+
[options="autowidth", frame=none, grid=none, cols="^,<"]
|===
| ♠6 | Phoenix <- Treat this Phoenix as a ♠6.
| ♥2 |
| ♣K | Phoenix <- Treat this Phoenix as a ♣0.
| ♦3 |
| ✿1 |
|===


== End of the game

When all 50 cards have been placed in the grid, and all 10 columns have been filled, you have all won the game.
If you cannot accomplish this, you have all lost the game.


== Rules regarding player discussion

No player may show their hand to another player.
You may not say or give hints about the numbers of the cards in your hand.
However, you may reveal how many cards you have in a suit.
You may also suggest a suit that you would like to be played.


== Hints

* For every column, it can be useful to discuss who places which suit, and who will play the Reset card.
* The 1s can only be placed in the first column or after a Reset (K) card, so try to play them as soon as possible.
* Except in the 10th column, try to avoid placing two 8s in the same column.
