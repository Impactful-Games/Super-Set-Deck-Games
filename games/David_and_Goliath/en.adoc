= David & Goliath
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 45 cards: 1-9 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ★.
* 15 more cards with 4 players or more: 10-12 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ★.
* 15 more cards with 5 players or more: 13-15 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ★.
* 15 more cards with 6 players: 16-18 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ★.
* Paper and a pencil to keep the score.
****

In David & Goliath, the high cards are the strongest but somewhat clumsy, and the low cards are the weakest but can be played cleverly.

Each player plays a card in turn.
The player with the lowest card wins the highest card.
The player with the highest card wins all the other cards.

At the end of the round, you score points in each suit.
If you have only one or two cards of a suit, each of them scores its value, which can be very lucrative.
If you have more than two cards of a suit, each of them scores only 1 point.


[#setup]
== Setup

Depending on the number of players, use only some of the cards:

* With 3 players, use only the cards of value 1-9 (45 cards).
* With 4 players, use only the cards of value 1-12 (60 cards).
* With 5 players, use only the cards of value 1-15 (75 cards).
* With 6 players, use all the cards (90 cards).

Put the other cards aside.
Shuffle the cards and deal them all out evenly: each player receives 15 cards, which they take in hand.


== Playing a round

The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick.

They play any card from their hand face up in front of them.
Then, clockwise, every other player must play a card face up in front of them.
You must always follow suit: play a card of the suit of the first card if you can.
If you cannot follow suit, you may play a card of any other suit.

Once everyone has played a card, first determine the highest card, then the lowest card:

* The highest card is always the one with the highest value, whatever its suit.
If several players played the highest value, the card played last is the highest.
* The lowest card is always the one with the lowest value, whatever its suit.
If several players played the lowest value, the card played last is the lowest.
* If all the cards have the same value, the card played last is the highest, and the card played second to last is the lowest.
* The player of the lowest card wins the highest card.
They place it face up next to them on the table.
* The player of the highest card wins all the other cards.
They place them face up next to them on the table, sorted by suit.

The player of the highest card leads the next trick in the same way.
The round goes on until 15 tricks have been played and everyone has played all 15 cards of their hand.

NOTE: During the round, everyone wins more and more cards.
Keep the cards you win next to you on the table, sorted by suit, so that every single card can be seen.

.Example of a trick
====
* Frank leads ♣4: ♣ must now be followed.
* Sabine plays ♣9.
* Peter plays ♥2, having no ♣ card.
* Andrea plays ♣7.

Sabine's card is the highest (♣9), and Peter's card is the lowest (♥2).

Peter wins the highest card, Sabine's ♣9, and places it face up on the table.

Sabine wins all the other cards and places them face up on the table, sorted by suit.

Sabine now leads the next trick with any card.
====


[#scoring]
== Scoring

At the end of the round, each player scores points for all the cards they won during the round.
Each suit is scored separately:

* If you have only one or two cards of a suit, each of them scores its value.
* If you have more than two cards of a suit, each of them scores only 1 point.

Then add up the points you scored in the 5 suits: that is your score for the round.

.Example of scoring
====
At the end of the round, Frank has won these cards:

* ♠4, ♠10 & ♠11: 3 points.
* ♥8: 8 points.
* ♣5 & ♣9: 14 points.
* ♦2, ♦3, ♦6, ♦10 & ♦12: 5 points.
* ★10 & ★12: 22 points.

Frank scores a total of 52 points!
====


== End of the game

After each round, write down the score of each player.
The deal then passes clockwise.
Play as many rounds as there are players.
The player with the most points at the end of the game wins.
