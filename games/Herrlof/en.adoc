= Herrlof

.Components
****
* 36 numbered cards: 1-9 in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
* 6 special cards: 3 Wolves & 3 Butterflies.
* Paper and a pencil to write down the predictions and the scores.
****

Scandinavia during the Viking age, from roughly 800 to 1050 AD, is characterized by powerful magnates (or chieftains) and kings.
The title of king is not inherited: you fight for it.
The leading magnate is recognized by the others as “first among equals”.
Four magnates now battle for Herrlof: the victory and praise gained in war.
Who will become the next Viking king?


== Game overview

In this trick-taking game, your goal is to be the first player to reach 50 points or more: the player with the most points wins.
You score points by winning tricks, and you can gain bonuses.
The game is played over a maximum of 10 rounds, although players most often reach 50 points well before that.


== Preparation

Each player takes a sheet of paper to keep track of their predictions and score.
For the first round, randomly pick one player as the dealer; the role of dealer then passes to the next player at each following round.


== Gameplay

The game is played in rounds.
Each round, you:

1. deal 15 cards;
2. set the trump;
3. predict your score;
4. battle for tricks;
5. reveal the predictions and determine your score.

Every round consists of 15 tricks, and a trick consists of one card played by each player.


=== 1. Dealing cards & 2. Setting the trump

The dealer deals 15 cards to each player, and the remaining 12 cards form a face-down deck on the side of the table.
The dealer then turns the top card of the deck face up: the suit of this card is the trump suit for this round.
If this is a Wolf or a Butterfly, there is no trump suit.


=== 3. Predict your score

Both players take their dealt cards into their hand, and predict how many tricks they think they will win.
They write this down on their sheet and keep it concealed.
You are not allowed to change your prediction during a round, and you cannot show your prediction to the other player.


=== 4. Battle for tricks

The player next to the dealer starts the first trick by playing one card: let’s call them player A.
The card played by player A indicates which suit player B must play, if player B has that suit.
If player B does not have a card of that suit, they may play any card.
You are always allowed to play a Wolf or a Butterfly instead of following suit.
If a player starts a trick with a Wolf or a Butterfly, the other player may play any card.

.Trump: ♠
====
* Player A plays ♣7; player B doesn’t have ♣ cards (anymore), and therefore plays any card.
Player B plays ♥8.
Player A wins the trick.
* Player A plays ♣7; player B doesn’t have ♣ cards (anymore), and therefore plays any card.
Player B plays ♠3.
Player B wins the trick, since ♠ is the trump suit.
====

Each trick is won by a single player.
If both played cards are of the same suit, the player with the higher card wins the trick.
If player B played a different suit than player A, then player A wins the trick (regardless of the number on the cards), unless player B played a trump card.
If player B played a trump card (while player A played another suit), player B wins the trick, regardless of the number on their card.

The winner of the trick takes both cards, places them on top of each other, and puts them to the side of the table.
They place every following trick they win so that both players can see at any time how many tricks each player has won.
You are only allowed to review the most recent trick: you cannot check which cards were played in any previous trick.
The winner of a trick starts the next trick, unless a card with a special ability changes this.


=== 5. Reveal predictions and determine your score

When all the dealt cards have been played, each player reveals their prediction.
You receive one point for each trick you won.
If you won the exact number of tricks you predicted, you receive 10 bonus points.
If you only won three, four or five tricks, you receive 5 bonus points (whether or not this was your prediction).


== End of the game

The game ends when, after scoring a round, one or both players have reached 50 points.
The player with the most points wins.
In case of a tie, the player who most often received the prediction bonus wins.
If there is still a tie, the player with the most tricks in the final round wins.
If there is still a tie, the players share the win.


== Cards with special abilities

* *Wolf*: destroys the trick in which it is played.
This means that both cards played in this trick are removed from the game for this round, lowering the total number of tricks that can be won this round.
The player who started the trick starts the next trick.
If two Wolves are played in one trick, a new trump suit is immediately turned face up from the deck (replacing the existing trump).
* *Butterfly*: this card is lower than all other cards.
The loser of this trick starts the next trick.
However, if two Butterflies are played, the first one wins the trick.
* *1*: if you win the trick with this card, you may choose to steal a trick from the other player.
Note that this must be a trick they have already won.
* *3*: if you play this card, you may choose to take the top (face-down) card of the deck into your hand, and then discard a card from your hand to the bottom of the deck.
You may discard the card you just received.
This special ability applies regardless of who wins the trick.
* *6*: if you win the trick with this card, you may choose to blindly steal one card from the hand of the other player.
You may add this card to your hand and give a different card back to the other player, or hand back the same card you just stole.
* *9*: if you win the trick with this card, your opponent starts the next trick.

.Trump: ♥
====
* Player A plays ♥5, player B plays a Wolf.
The trick is destroyed, and player A starts the next trick.
* Player A plays ♠1, player B plays a Butterfly.
Player A wins the trick and takes one additional trick from player B, so player A adds two tricks in total to their pile.
Player B starts the next trick.
* Player A plays ♥3, takes the top face-down card of the deck, and discards another card to the bottom of the deck.
Player B plays ♥6.
Player B wins the trick, steals one card from player A’s hand, and gives back a different card from their own hand.
Player B starts the next trick.
* Player A plays ♣9, player B plays ♦4.
Player A wins the trick, and player B starts the next trick.
====


== Three-player variant

It is possible (and enjoyable) to play the game with three players.
Simply deal 11 cards to each player per round.
When a card with a special ability mentions “the other player”, read it as “another player”.
With the 9, the player to the left of the winner starts the next trick.
Note that the 5 bonus points for 3, 4 or 5 tricks are canceled with three players.
You are also advised to set the scoring goal at 40 points, rather than 50.
