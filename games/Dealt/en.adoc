= Dealt!
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 48 number cards: 1-12 in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
* 6 special cards: 2 Butterflies, 2 Monkeys & 2 Wolves.
* Paper and a pencil to keep the penalty points.
****

All of you are trying to play your cards as quickly as possible by outbidding the other players' combinations.
You may play one, two or three cards each time, but only if they are next to each other in your hand!

Once you get rid of all your cards, you are safe: the last player who still has cards gets a penalty point, and the first player with three penalty points loses the game!
If you fancy a longer game, you can play up to 4 penalty points.


[#setup]
== Setup

The oldest player shuffles all the cards and deals *10 cards* to each player (*7 cards* with 5 players).

[CAUTION]
====
*This is the most important rule of the game!*
Do not take your cards in hand until you have all ten (or seven) cards in front of you.
Then pick them up and fan them out, but *do not* change their order.
During the game, *no one* may change the order of the cards in their hand.
====

Once all the players have received their cards, the oldest player also deals two reserve cards face up in front of each player.
Put the remaining cards in the middle of the table as a face-down draw pile.


== Playing the game

The game is played in several rounds, each made of several tricks.
A round ends when a player gets a penalty point.
The player to the left of the dealer opens the first trick.


=== What is a trick?

One after another, clockwise, each of you plays one card combination from your hand.
A combination may include one, two or three cards, but *you can only play cards that are next to each other in your hand*.

If you open a trick, you can play any card combination.
The following players *must* outbid the last combination played.


=== What does "outbid" mean?

To outbid, you *must* play a card combination that ranks higher than the highest combination played so far: either the same combination with higher values, or a higher combination.

*The card combinations rank in this order, from the lowest to the highest:*

. *Solo*: a single card.
. *2-straight*: two cards of consecutive values (for example 2 & 3).
. *Pair*: two cards of the same value.
. *3-straight*: three cards of consecutive values (for example 3, 4 & 5).
. *Trips*: three cards of the same value.

[IMPORTANT]
====
The two or three cards of your combination must be next to each other in your hand.
Their order does not matter, however: 8-10-9 is just as good a 3-straight as 9-8-10 or 8-9-10.
====

.Example
====
Andy plays an 8 as a Solo. +
Carla outbids the Solo by playing a 12. +
Nico outbids it with a 2-straight, made of a 5 and a 4 that are *next to each other* in hand. +
Susie outbids it with a higher 2-straight, made of a 5 and a 6.
====


=== When do the reserve cards come into play?

If you cannot, or do not want to, outbid a combination, you *must* instead take *one* of your reserve cards in hand.
You can place it anywhere you want in your hand.
You may only take your own reserve cards, never those of other players.

.Example
====
Susie plays a pair of 11s. +
Andy outbids it with a 3-straight: 6, 7 & 8. +
Carla cannot outbid it, and must take a reserve card.
Carla chooses the 7 and places it next to a 6 in hand, to play both as a 2-straight in a later trick. +
Nico cannot outbid it either, and takes a reserve card: the 12.
====


=== Who wins a trick?

Once every player has either played a card combination *or* taken a reserve card, the trick ends.
Put all the cards played in the trick on a common discard pile.
The player who played the highest combination wins the trick and opens the next one.

*Special case*: if the winner of the trick has no cards left in hand (reserve cards do not count), the player who played the second-highest combination opens the next trick.
If the winner of the trick was the only player who played a combination, their right-hand neighbor opens the next trick.

IMPORTANT: The point of the game is not to win tricks, but to get rid of the cards in your hand as fast as possible.


=== The special cards

*The Butterfly* can take any value from 1 to 12.
You may play it on its own or as part of a combination.
However, like any other card, it can only be part of a combination with the cards next to it in your hand.
When you play a Butterfly, announce the value it takes.

*The Monkey* can only be played on its own.
It has no rank, so it does not need to outbid anything: you can play it whenever it is your turn.
When you play a Monkey, you immediately end and win the trick, even if some players have not played yet.
If you opened the trick with the Monkey and it was your last card, your right-hand neighbor opens the next trick.

*The Wolf* can only be played on its own too.
It has no rank, so you never need to outbid anyone to play it: you can always play it.
The next player must then outbid the combination played before your Wolf, or take a reserve card.
Both Wolves can be played in the same trick.
If you win a trick that includes a Wolf, draw three cards from the draw pile (six cards if both Wolves were played), one at a time, and place each of them anywhere you want in your hand.

.Example
====
Andy plays a 12 as a Solo. +
Carla plays a 2-straight: 7 & 6. +
Nico plays a 2-straight: 11 & 12. +
Susie plays a Wolf. +
Nico wins the trick.
Because of Susie's Wolf, Nico must draw three cards from the draw pile, one at a time, and place them in hand.
Then Nico opens the next trick.
====

If you open a trick with a Wolf, the next player can play any card combination or take a reserve card.
If all the other players take a reserve card (or if one of them plays the other Wolf), you win the trick.

If you win a trick that includes a Wolf, you must apply its effect and draw cards even if you had played your last card.
You stay in the round with the cards you drew.


=== End of a round

If you have no cards left in hand at the end of a trick (your reserve cards do not count), you are out of the current round: you can no longer lose it!
A round ends when only one player has cards left in hand at the end of a trick.
A round also ends if, during a trick, a player cannot outbid the highest combination *and* has no reserve card left to take.
In either case, that player loses the round and gets a penalty point.

Several players may get a penalty point at the same time, at the end of a trick.
This happens when all the players still in the round end up with no cards in hand after a trick.
In this case, all the players who took part in this last trick get a penalty point, except its winner.


=== The next round

The oldest player shuffles all the cards again and deals them as explained in <<setup>>.
The player who got the penalty point opens the first trick of the new round.
If several players got a penalty point at the end of the last round, the oldest player opens the first trick of the new round.


== End of the game

When a player gets their 3rd penalty point (or their 4th in a longer game), they lose the game.
If this happens to several players at the same time, they all lose.

All the other players can consider themselves the lucky winners!
