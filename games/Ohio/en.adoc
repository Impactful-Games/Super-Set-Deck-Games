= Ohio
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 11 cards per player, one suit each: 1-10 & K.
** With 2 players: in ♠ & ♥.
** With 3 players: in ♠, ♥ & ♣.
** With 4 players: in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
** With 5 players: in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦ & ✿.
* Paper and a pencil to keep the score.
****

Ohio is an exciting card game where cards with lower values are stronger than cards with higher values.
All players start with exactly the same cards.
Players take turns to play cards of ever lower values.
The player who finally plays the lowest card wins the trick.
In the end, the player with the highest total value of cards wins the game.

CAUTION: For this game, the suits of the cards do not matter: only their values count.


== Setup

Each player receives a complete set of cards in one suit: the values 1 to 10 and one K.
Hold your cards in your hand.
Choose at random who plays the first card, then play clockwise.


== Play

The game is about taking tricks and winning high-value cards.
On your turn, either play a card lower than the previous one, or pass.
You may pass for tactical reasons, even if you are able to play.
After passing, you may not play any more cards in the current trick.

The cards do not have to be consecutive, as long as they are lower.
For example, if the previous player played a 9, you can play an 8, or anything lower, such as a 2 or even a 1.
It is quite possible for players to have more than one turn in the same trick.

If, at the beginning of your turn, your own card is still the last card played, all the other players have passed, and you win the trick.
Place its cards face down in front of you, then play the first card of the next trick.
Each player must be able to see all the cards of the current trick.
To do so, place them side by side on the table.
When you play a card, call out the current value of the trick.
The value of the trick is the sum of the values of all its cards.
Each K counts minus 10.
For example, after a 10 and an 8, a player plays a 4 and calls out “22”.

A K may be played after any card, even after another K.
It is always a bit lower than the previous card.
For example, a 4 may be followed by a K, then by another K, then by a 3.
If a trick starts with a K, any card may follow it.


== End of the game

When a player plays their last card, the current trick is still played out.
Then the game ends.

Each player adds up the values of the cards in their tricks, and subtracts the values of the cards left in their hand.
Each K counts minus 10, whether it is in a trick or in your hand.
The player with the highest score wins.

The easiest way to calculate your score is to add up all the cards in your hand and all your K cards.
Then cancel out this total with your other cards.
Then add up your remaining cards to get your score.

Play several games in a row.
Write down the result of each player after each game.
In the end, the player with the highest total score wins.
