= Fool!
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* Suit cards, depending on the number of players (see <<references>>):
** 4 players: 45 cards, 0-15 in ★, 0-11 in ♠, 0-9 in ♥ & 0-6 in ♣.
** 5 players: 56 cards, 0-18 in ★, 0-14 in ♠, 0-12 in ♥ & 0-8 in ♣.
** 6 players: 67 cards, 0-18, J, Q & S in ★, 0-17 in ♠, 0-15 in ♥ & 0-10 in ♣.
** 7 players: 78 cards, 0-18, J, Q, S, K, C & A in ★, 0-18, J & Q in ♠, 0-18 in ♥ & 0-12 in ♣.
** 8 players: 82 cards, 0-18, J, Q, S, K, C, A & -‽ in ★, 0-18, J, Q & S in ♠, 0-18 & J in ♥ & 0-13 in ♣.
* Wild cards: 3 Butterflies with 4 players, 4 with 5 players, 5 with 6 players, 6 with 7 or 8 players.
* Fool! cards: 1 Monkey (2 Monkeys with 7 or 8 players).
* Paper and a pencil to keep the score.
****

In Fool!, you try to get rid of all your cards quickly.
You do not need to win every trick, but your goal should be to play a card in every trick.
If you play the *worst card* in a trick, you are befooled!
You must pass during the next trick and cannot get rid of a card.


== Setup

. Take the cards matching your number of players, as shown in its <<references,reference>>: the suit cards, the Butterflies and the Monkeys.
Put the other cards back in the box.
. Place the Monkey in the center of the table (both Monkeys with 7 or 8 players).
. Shuffle the cards and deal them evenly to all players, so that each player receives 12 cards in their hand.
With 8 players, each player only receives 11 cards.
. You will need paper and a pencil for scoring.
. The player to the left of the dealer is the start player of the first round.


== Playing the game

In each round, you play several tricks, until one or more players have emptied their hand by playing their last card.

The start player begins the trick by playing any card from their hand.
The suit of this card is the *lead suit*.
Following the start player, in clockwise order, each opponent also plays a card.
If you have a card of the lead suit in your hand, you must play it: this is *following the lead suit*.
If you have no card of the lead suit in your hand, you are forced to play a card of another suit: this is *shedding a suit*.

The player who played the highest value in the lead suit wins the trick.
In each suit, the cards all have different values, so there is always a definitive winner of a trick.
The figures rank above 18, in the order given by the <<references,reference>> of your number of players.

The player who played the *worst card* is befooled!
They take a Monkey and must sit out and pass during the next trick.
With 7 or 8 players, the two players who played the worst cards each take one of the Monkeys, and both must pass!


=== Determining the worst card

* If all players follow the lead suit, playing cards of the same suit, the worst card is the one with the lowest value.
* If one or more cards are shed, the shed card with the lowest value is the worst card.
If several shed cards have the same lowest value, the last one played is the worst card.
This can happen when players shed cards of different suits.

The winner of the trick places the trick face down to the side, and begins the next trick by playing any card from their hand.

*Don't be the Fool!*
The players who have a Monkey are not allowed to play a card during the current trick, but they are free to play in the following trick.

During the entire round, all players must hold their cards in such a way that all players can check the number of cards in their hand!


=== Beware of the Butterflies!

The Butterflies are wild and always adopt the lead suit.
Thus, you always follow the lead suit when you play a Butterfly!

You *may* play a Butterfly even if you have cards of the lead suit in your hand.
You *do not have to* play a Butterfly if you have no card of the lead suit left in your hand.

A Butterfly is worth -1: it is the lowest card of the lead suit.
With this in mind, the challenge of the game is to try to play your Butterflies in the same trick as other players who are shedding.

If the first player plays a Butterfly, the next card played determines the lead suit to be followed.
If that second card is also a Butterfly, the third card played determines the lead suit, and so on.
In rare cases, if all players play a Butterfly, the first player wins the trick and the last player is befooled!


=== Examples

Anne, Paul, Rachel and Camille play a game of Fool!.
Camille was befooled and took the Monkey in the last trick.

.Example A
====
Anne plays ♣5 first, Paul follows with ♣3 and Rachel with ♣6, and Camille passes.
Paul played the worst card and is befooled.
Rachel wins the trick with the highest card.
====

.Example B
====
Rachel plays ♥8 first, Camille sheds ★3 and Anne follows with ♥0, while Paul passes.
Camille is the only player who must shed a suit, and is befooled.
Rachel wins this trick again.
====

.Example C
====
Again, Rachel plays ♣7 first, Anne sheds ★12 and Paul sheds ♠9, while Camille passes.
Paul is befooled, as compared to Anne, Paul is shedding the lower valued card.
Rachel wins the third trick in a row.
====

.Example D
====
Rachel plays ♥6 first, Camille plays a Butterfly and Anne follows with ♥9, while Paul passes.
Camille's Butterfly follows ♥.
Still, Camille is befooled, as Anne follows ♥ with the highest value of the lead suit, and even wins the trick.
====

.Example E
====
Anne plays ★8 first, Paul follows ★ with a Butterfly, and Rachel sheds ♥1, while Camille passes.
Rachel is befooled, takes the Monkey and must pass during the next trick.
====


== End of the round

The round ends after one or more players have emptied their hand by playing their last card.
In this last trick, the players still need to take the Monkeys.


[#scoring]
== Scoring

You score negative points if you have cards remaining in your hand:

* For each suit card, you score 2 negative points plus its value: ♥0 costs 2 points, ★12 costs 14 points.
The values of the figures are given in the <<references,reference>> of your number of players.
* For each Butterfly, you score 5 negative points.

If you have emptied your hand by playing your last card on the last trick, you score 10 positive points, unless you are befooled and took a Monkey in that last trick: then you score 0 points.

.Example
====
At the end of the round, Anne still has ★1, ★7 and a Butterfly, and scores 3 + 9 + 5 = 17 negative points.
Rachel still has ♥7 and ♣2, and scores 9 + 4 = 13 negative points.
Camille and Paul both played all their cards, but because of the worst card Paul played, Paul is befooled and took the Monkey.
Camille scores 10 positive points, and Paul scores 0 points.
====


== The next round

The previous start player is now the dealer and shuffles all the cards.
The new start player is the player with the most negative points.
In case of a tie, the tied player closest in clockwise order to the previous start player becomes the new start player.


== End of the game

The game ends when either:

* any player has scored 80 negative points or more,
* or 10 positive points have been scored 6 or more times, by any or all players: just make a tally on your score sheet.

The player with the most points wins Fool!
This may be the player with negative points closest to zero.
