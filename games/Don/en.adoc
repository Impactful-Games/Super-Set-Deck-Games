= Don!
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 30 numbered cards: 0-9 in ♠, ♥ & ♣.
* 12 Money cards per player:
** 3 players: 36 cards, 1-12 in ♦, ✿ & ★.
** 4 players: 48 cards, add 13-18, J, Q, S & 3 K in ♦.
** 5 players: 60 cards, add 13-18, J, Q, S & 3 K in ★.
** 6 players: 72 cards, add ♦0, ✿0, ✿J, ✿Q, 3 ✿K, ★0, ★19-21 & ★C.
* _Serengeti variant_: 1 more Money card per player, 1-3 in ⎈ with 3 players, 1-4 with 4 players, 1-5 with 5 players, 1-6 with 6 players.
****

The numbered cards are auctioned off among the players, in lots of 1, 2 or 3 cards.
Score the most points by buying numbered cards, preferably many of the same suit.

[CAUTION]
====
In these rules, "*Card*" refers to the face-up numbered cards, while "*Money*" refers to the face-down Money cards.
The Money cards are always used face down, whatever their suit and value: each of them is worth 1 money.
====


== Setup

Choose a start player at random.
This player shuffles the numbered cards, then places them face down in the middle of the table: this is the draw pile.
Each player takes 12 money.
During the game, you may keep secret how much money you have.


== Playing the game

The game is played over 15 rounds, each round being divided into two phases:

. Turn over cards.
. Auction these cards.

After 15 rounds, all the cards have been auctioned.


=== 1. Turn over cards

In each round, a different number of cards is revealed: one card in the first round, two cards in the second round, three cards in the third round, then repeat this pattern.
So: 1 - 2 - 3 - 1 - 2 - 3 - and so on.


=== 2. Auction these cards

The revealed cards are now sold together, as one lot.
The start player makes the first bid, of any amount.
The next player in clockwise order must make a higher bid or drop out of this auction.
Then the next player in clockwise order either raises or drops out, and so on.
A player who has dropped out of an auction cannot take part in it again.
When all players but one have dropped out, the high bidder places the cards face up in front of them, then pays money equal to their bid.

If no player bids on the lot, the start player takes these cards for free.


[#bid-restrictions]
==== Bid restrictions

You may not bid more than you can pay.
If you do, you must discard one of the cards you have won as a penalty: this card is removed from the game.
The revealed cards are then auctioned again, and you may not take part in this auction.

An important restriction applies to bids: you may not make a bid that matches the numbers on the cards you have already won.
This includes any two-digit bid whose last digit matches these numbers.

Anyone who makes such a bid must pay 1 money to the Pot as a penalty (if they have money), even if they correct themselves immediately.
The incorrect bid is withdrawn, and the player must make a new bid or pass.

.Possible bids
====
Anke owns cards with the numbers 4 and 6.
So Anke may not bid 4, 6, 14, 16, and so on.
====

These are the only restrictions: the opening bid and the raises may be of any amount.


=== Who gets the money?

Once the price of a lot is set, you must determine who gets the money.
Two factors play a role here: the winning bid, and the numbers on the cards that the players have previously won.
If the bid matches the number on a card you own, you receive a share of the money.

.Single player distribution
====
Anke buys a lot for 7 money.
Bert owns the only card with the number 7, so Bert takes the 7 money from Anke.
====

Players who own the same number of cards with a matching number receive an equal share of the winning bid.

.Multiple players distribution
====
Dieter buys two cards for 9 money.
Anke, Bert and Claus each own one card with the number 9.
Dieter pays Anke, Bert and Claus 3 money each.
====

A player who owns more cards with a matching number than anyone else receives the whole winning bid alone.

.Majority player distribution
====
Anke buys a card for 8 money.
Bert owns two cards with the number 8, and Claus one.
The 8 money go to Bert, who owns more cards with the number 8 than Claus.
====

If no one owns a card with a matching number, the money is split evenly among all the players other than the buyer.

.Even distribution
====
Anke buys a card for 6 money.
No one owns a card with the number 6, so the money is split evenly among all the players other than Anke.
====

IMPORTANT: The winner of the auction never receives any of the money being distributed.


==== Two-digit numbers

If the winning bid is a two-digit number, its last digit determines how the money is distributed.
So, on a bid of 14, the number to match is 4; on a bid of 10, it is 0; and so on.

.Two-digit distribution
====
Claus buys three cards for 12 money.
Bert owns the only card with a 2.
Since only the last digit counts with two-digit bids, Bert receives the 12 money.
====


==== Remainders

The bid cannot always be divided evenly.
When this happens, round down to distribute the money evenly.
Place the remaining money in the middle of the table (in the Pot): it is distributed with the payment of the next auction.

.Distribution with remainder
====
Anke must pay 13 money to Bert and Claus.
They receive 6 money each, and the remaining money goes to the Pot.
In the next round, Bert must pay 9 money to Anke and Claus.
With the money of the Pot added in, each of them receives 5 money.
====


== Next round

Whoever won the auction becomes the new start player.
Repeat the two phases: turning over cards, and auctioning them.


== Side auctions

If you run into financial difficulty, you may sell one of your cards, and only one, in a side auction.
Do it at the start of a round, before new cards are turned face up.
Since you sell one of your own cards, you receive all the money.


== End of the game

After all the cards have been auctioned, players score points.
The more cards you have of the same suit, the more points you score:

* 1 card of a suit: 1 point.
* 2 cards of the same suit: 3 points.
* 3 cards of the same suit: 6 points.
* 4 cards of the same suit: 10 points.
* 5 cards of the same suit: 15 points.
* The player with the most money scores 2 points.
If several players tie for the most money, they each score 2 points.

The player with the most points wins.
In case of a tie, the tied player with the highest sum of numbers on their cards wins.


== Variants

=== Invasions

At the start of each round, before new cards are turned over, play an additional Exchange phase.
(At the beginning of the game, no one owns any cards, so skip this phase.)

The start player goes first and announces whether they exchange a card.
If they do, they take a card from an opponent and place it in front of them, then give that opponent one of their own cards.
The opponent cannot prevent it.
Then the next player in clockwise order may exchange, and so on.
After everyone has had the opportunity to exchange, at most once per round, turn over the new cards.

The players who exchanged a card (but not the players who underwent an exchange) may not take part in the auction of this round!
If all the players exchanged cards, there is no auction, and the start player takes the revealed cards for free.


=== Serengeti

This variant follows the rules of Serengeti, the new edition of Don!

* Each player starts with 10 money (instead of 12).
The remaining Money cards, 3 per player, form the bank.
* Whenever you pass without having bid on the lot, take 2 money from the bank (or what is left in the bank, if it holds fewer).
* The winning bid is distributed proportionally: divide it by the number of cards with the matching number, rounding down; each owner receives this amount for each matching card they own.
The remaining money goes to the bank.
+
.Proportional distribution
====
Anke buys a card for 8 money.
Bert owns two cards with the number 8, and Claus one.
Bert receives 4 money, and Claus 2.
The remaining 2 money go to the bank.
====
* The penalty for an incorrect bid (see <<bid-restrictions>>) is paid to the bank.
* The player with the most money at the end of the game scores 3 points (instead of 2).
* If there is a tie for points, the tied player with the most cards wins.
If there is still a tie, the tied player with the most money wins.
