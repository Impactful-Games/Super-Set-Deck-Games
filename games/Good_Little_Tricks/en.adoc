The game is played over a fixed number of hands.
Players score points at the end of each hand, and the winner of the game is the player with the *lowest* total score at the end of the game.


== How to play

Good Little Tricks uses many of the conventions of standard trick-taking games such as Whist, so if you are familiar with these games, playing this game should be straightforward.

The main difference between Good Little Tricks and other trick-taking games is that the winner of each trick does not automatically claim the cards in the trick.
Instead, all the cards played in a trick are first put face up into a ‘Display’ beside the play area.

Then, before the next trick is played, the players check whether all the cards of any suit are now in the Display.
If they are, the winner of the trick must claim all the cards of this suit (which are not necessarily all the cards that were played in the trick).

At the end of each hand, each player scores a number of points equal to the number of cards they have claimed.
However, if a single player claims all 27 cards, they have ‘swept the board’ and each *other* player receives a penalty of 27 points.

NOTE: Remember: the player with the lowest score at the end of the game wins.


=== Game length

The game is played over a fixed number of hands.
With 2 or 3 players, play two hands per player.
With 4 or 5 players, play one hand per player.

* *2 players*: play 4 hands.
* *3 players*: play 6 hands.
* *4 players*: play 4 hands.
* *5 players*: play 5 hands.

Choose a player to shuffle and deal the cards for the first hand.
The player to the dealer’s left is always the start player and leads the first trick.

Players take turns clockwise to be the dealer, so that every player is the start player and leads the first trick the same number of times.

NOTE: The <<two-players,changes for 2 players>> are explained below.


== Dealing

At the start of each hand, the dealer shuffles the 27 numbered cards and deals them out evenly, so that each player receives the same number of cards:

* *3 players*: 9 cards each.
* *4 players*: 6 cards each (3 spare cards).
* *5 players*: 5 cards each (2 spare cards).

Beside the play area, place the 6 A cards in a line in this order: ★, ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦, ✿.
This is the Display.

Put any spare cards face up in the Display, below their matching A card.


=== Adding cards to the Display

When cards are added to the Display, arrange them so that the cards of the same suit are placed together below their matching A card.

Overlap the cards of the same suit in a vertical stack, so that all their values remain visible.


=== Trumps

Unlike most trick-taking games, the trump suit changes during the game, so watch out!

At any time, the trump suit is the *smallest* suit of which at least one card remains in the players’ hands (the leftmost visible A card).
Once all the cards of this suit have been played, flip the matching A card face down: from the next trick on, the trump suit is the next smallest suit that still has unplayed cards.

Therefore, at the start of a hand, ★ is almost always the trump suit, unless both ★ cards were dealt to the Display in a 4- or 5-player game.
In this rare case, ♠ is the trump suit.


== Playing tricks

The player to the left of the dealer is the start player and leads the first trick.

When you lead a trick, choose a card from your hand and play it face up in front of you.
Its suit is the led suit.
You *cannot* lead a trump, unless you only have trumps in your hand and so have no other choice.

Going clockwise, each other player must then play one card in turn, and must ‘follow suit’ if possible: you must play a card of the led suit.

If you cannot follow suit, you may play any card from your hand, including a trump if you have one.

Once each player has played a card, the winner of the trick is determined.
If no trump was played, the winner is the player who played the highest card of the led suit.
If one or more trumps were played, the winner is the player who played the highest trump.

After each trick, move all the cards played into the Display, arranging them by suit.


=== Claiming cards from the Display

After each trick, check whether any suit is ‘complete’: that is, whether all of its cards are now in the Display.
The winner of the trick must claim all the cards of every complete suit, and flip their matching A cards face down.
Leave all the cards of incomplete suits in the Display.
Keep the cards you claim in front of you.

Remember to keep track of the trump suit.
The trump suit is always the smallest incomplete suit (the leftmost visible A card).


=== Leading the next trick

The winner of each trick must now lead the next trick.


== Scoring

Keep playing until everyone has played all their cards and all the cards have been claimed from the Display.
Each player’s score is equal to the number of cards they claimed during the hand.
However, if a single player claimed all 27 cards, they have ‘swept the board’: that player scores no points, and each other player receives a penalty of 27 points.

Write down the scores, and keep track of each player’s total score from hand to hand.
The game ends after the set number of hands has been played.

The player with the lowest total score is the winner.

== Changes for 2 players

Deal 9 cards face down to each player, plus 9 cards to an imaginary third player called the ‘Dummy’.
Place the Dummy’s cards in a face-down stack between the players, as if the Dummy was seated to the dealer’s left.

Flip the top *three cards* of the Dummy’s stack face up and arrange them from left to right in suit order: the cards of the smallest suit first, and the cards of larger suits to their right.

If there are 2 or 3 cards of the same suit, place the lower cards first.
Always keep the Dummy’s face-up cards arranged in this way.
This is the Dummy’s ‘line’.
After each trick, flip a new card from the Dummy’s stack and add it to the line.


=== Playing Dummy cards

The Dummy takes its turn as if it was a third player, automatically playing one card from its line.

* The Dummy must follow suit if possible, and always plays its highest card of that suit if it has a choice.
* If the Dummy cannot follow suit, it always plays the *leftmost* card of its line.
+
NOTE: This card is a trump if the Dummy has any face up.
* The Dummy can win tricks and claim cards from complete suits in the Display, just like a regular player.
If it wins a trick, it leads the next trick by playing the *rightmost* card of its line.
