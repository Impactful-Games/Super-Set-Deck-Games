= Fiasco
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 42 numbered cards: 1-4 & 6-8 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦, ✿ & ★.
* 6 suit cards: the A of ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦, ✿ & ★.
* 6 Wolves.
* 6 Butterflies, the Fiasco cards.
****

The goal of Fiasco is to collect all the cards of one suit by winning tricks, to score the most points, while preventing your opponents from doing the same.


== Setup

Deal a suit card to each player, who places it face up in front of them.
This card shows the suit this player collects.


== Playing a round

Deal six cards face down to each player.
Keep the remaining cards of the deck face down beside the dealer.
Everyone looks at the cards in their hand.

Then, starting with the player on the dealer's left and going clockwise, each player places one card from their hand in the middle of the table.
The player who placed the highest card wins the trick and every card in the middle (the "pot").
If the pot contains cards of the suit this player collects, they place these cards face up in their collection pile.
The collection pile lies on the table, just in front of its player, and is used to score points later in the game.
Sweep all the other cards of the pot into a waste pile.

The player who won the trick leads the next one, and play continues.
The highest card always wins the trick, and its winner always leads the next one.

NOTE: Suit cards cannot go directly from your hand into your collection pile.
You must first win them in a pot.

Play any card you want.
There is no trump, and all suits have the same rank.
You do not have to follow suit.
If you want to win the trick, play a high card.
Play a low card to avoid it.


=== Wolves

The Wolf is the highest card of the deck, and it also scores points, whatever the suit you collect.
If you win a pot containing a Wolf, place the Wolf in your collection pile: it scores points.


=== Butterflies (Fiasco cards)

A Butterfly has a rank of 5: half of the deck is higher, and half of the deck is lower.
If you win a trick containing Butterflies, you lose one card of your collection pile for each Butterfly in the pot.
If you must lose more cards than your collection pile holds, you only lose what it holds.
Losses do not carry over to the next trick.

Try to get rid of your Butterflies by inflicting them on your opponents.
Hence, commit a Fiasco!


=== Ties

If several players tie for the highest card, there is a "tie-for-high", and more cards must be played.
The cards in the middle stay in the pot, and have no bearing on the winner of the new trick.

Each player places another card in the middle to determine the winner of the new trick.
The winner of this trick wins all the cards in the pot.
If there is a second tie-for-high, play continues until there is a winner.

If a tie occurs with the last cards in the players' hands, deal more cards until a winner can be determined.
The only exception is a tie-for-high with the last cards before the Counting Round: ignore these cards, and sweep them into the waste pile before counting the points.


=== Re-deal

Once the first six cards have been played, the dealer deals six more cards from the deck to each player, and play continues.
If there are not enough cards in the deck, deal the same number of cards to each player, as many as possible, and play them out.
Discard any remaining cards into the waste pile.

When all the cards of the deck have been played, shuffle the waste pile to form a new deck, and deal it.
The dealer changes with each new deck, clockwise.

The deck is exhausted three times before players count their points.


=== Points

After the deck has been exhausted three times, play stops and players count the cards in their collection pile.
This is the Counting Round.
Each player scores:

* 2 points for each card of their suit they have collected.
* 1 point for each Wolf in their collection pile.
* 4 bonus points for having all six Wolves.
* 6 bonus points for having all seven cards of their suit.

After counting the points, gather all the cards and shuffle them.
Players keep collecting the same suit.
Add up the points of each Counting Round to get each player's overall score.


== Winning the game

*Quick game*: the player with the highest score after the first Counting Round wins.

*Extended game*: the first player to score 42 points or more wins the game.
