= Klunker
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 94 commodity cards:
** 1-14 in ♠, ♥, ♣, ♦, ✿ & ★.
** 10 Animals: 5 Butterflies & 5 Wolves.
* 3 to 5 purchase cards: 1-3 in ⎈, plus ⎈4 with 4 or 5 players and ⎈5 with 5 players.
* 1 starting player card: ⎈K.
****

The players display, purchase and sell commodities, trying to become the player with the most cash at the end of the game.

[CAUTION]
====
For this game, only the suits of the commodity cards matter: their values are ignored.
The 10 Butterflies & Wolves all belong to the same commodity type, the Animals.
====


== Setup

The youngest player takes the starting player card, ⎈K.
The starting player shuffles the 94 commodity cards and gives each player 1 card.
Each player places this card face down to the right of their commodity counter-top.
This is their starting cash.
Players may not look at the commodity side of their cash.

The starting player places 1 purchase card per player face up in the center of the table.
Thus, use ⎈1 to ⎈3 with 3 players, ⎈1 to ⎈4 with 4 players, and all 5 purchase cards with 5 players.
Then, they deal each player 6 commodity cards face down.
Finally, they place the commodity deck face down in the center of the table.

The players may now look at (and organize) their cards: these are their starting hands.

The playing area of each player should look like this at the start of the game:

[%autowidth,frame=none,grid=none]
|===
| | | | | | | | | | | | |
3.5+^.^h| A | | | | | | | 3.5+^.^h| C
| 5.3+^.^h| B |
| |
| |
| | | | | | |
|===

* *A*: commodity counter-top (face up).
* *B*: cash (face down).
* *C*: commodity cellar (face up).

TIP: If you have enough table space, you may place your commodity counter-top in a line above your cash and your cellar.

It is easiest to keep things in order if the cards on your counter-top face your opponents and the cards in your cellar face you.


== Game overview

The game is played in several rounds.
Each round consists of 3 phases, which are played in the following order:

1. Fill commodity counter-tops.
2. Store commodities in cellars.
3. Purchase commodities.


=== 1. Fill commodity counter-tops

The players place as many commodities as they want on their counter-tops.

Beginning with the starting player and following in clockwise order once around the table, the players may place commodity cards from their hands on their commodity counter-tops.
All the cards placed on counter-tops must be displayed openly: no card may be hidden by other cards.
On your turn, you may place any or all of the 6 cards in your hand on your counter-top.

IMPORTANT: A player who has no commodities on their counter-top must put at least 1 card there.
A player who has commodities on their counter-top may place more, but is not required to do so.
Each counter-top must have at least one commodity card at the end of this phase.
Otherwise, there is no limit to the number or type of cards on a counter-top.

The commodities on the counter-tops may be purchased later in the round by the other players or by the player who owns the counter-top.


=== 2. Store commodities in cellars

In contrast to putting commodities on counter-tops, the commodity cards are stored in the cellars one at a time.
The starting player begins.
They may store 1 card from their hand in their cellar, or pass.
Play continues in clockwise order, with each player choosing either to store 1 card in their cellar or to pass.
Play continues as long as at least one player wants and is able to store cards in their cellar.

The first player to pass and not put a card in their cellar takes purchase card ⎈1 and places it face up in their playing area.
The next player who passes takes purchase card ⎈2, and so on until all purchase cards have been taken.
When this occurs, the next phase of the round begins.
After a player has taken a purchase card, they take no further action in this phase of this round: they may not store any more cards in their cellar, nor take another purchase card.

You may only store cards in your own cellar.
Players store the cards in their cellars face up and sorted by commodity type, for all to see.
Cards may not be hidden by other cards.

NOTE: Players are not required to store any card in their cellars in a round, nor to have any card in their cellars.
Also, a player may store several cards in their cellar in a round.

As soon as a player has 4 cards of the same commodity type in their cellar, they must sell them, converting them to cash.


==== Selling commodities

If you only have these 4 cards in your cellar, take all 4 cards, turn them over and put them on your cash pile.
You have sold them for 4 banknotes.

If you have 2 types of commodities in your cellar when you sell a group of 4, you only receive 3 banknotes: turn 3 of the commodity cards over and put them on your cash pile.
Discard the 4th card on the discard pile.
If you have 3 types, you only receive 2 banknotes, and discard the other 2 cards.
Finally, if you have 4 or more types, you only receive 1 banknote, and discard the other 3 cards.
If you have more than 4 cards of a type, the extra cards count as another type.

IMPORTANT: *Exception*: when you sell cards from the set of 10 Animals, you always get 4 banknotes, regardless of the number of commodity types in your cellar.
As with all sales, you must sell exactly 4 Animal cards.

NOTE: As soon as a commodity type is sold from a cellar, the value of the remaining commodities increases.

.Value increase
====
A player has 3 types of commodities in their cellar, including a set of 4 ♦ commodities and a set of 4 ♠ commodities.
They choose to sell their 4 ♦ commodities first.
They receive 2 banknotes for them, since there are 3 types of commodities in their cellar.
They then sell the ♠ commodities and receive 3 banknotes, since they now only have 2 commodity types in their cellar.
====


=== 3. Purchase commodities from counter-tops

Beginning with the player who has purchase card ⎈1, then the player who has ⎈2, and so on, the players may purchase cards from commodity counter-tops.
On your turn, you may purchase the cards of any one (and only one) commodity counter-top, including your own.

When you purchase the cards of another player, pay this player 1 banknote from your cash, regardless of how many cards were on the counter-top.
When you purchase the cards of your own counter-top, you pay nothing.
In any case, you must take all the cards of the selected counter-top: a partial purchase is not allowed.

You must immediately put all the purchased cards in your cellar.
If this completes a set of 4 cards, immediately sell the set as described above.

If you have cards on your counter-top on your turn to purchase, you must purchase commodities, either from your own counter-top or from the counter-top of another player.

If you have no card on your counter-top, you may purchase commodities from another counter-top, or choose to purchase nothing.
If you choose to purchase nothing, the purchase phase ends immediately: the players who still have a purchase card may not make purchases this round.
Otherwise, the phase ends with the last player making a purchase.


== End of the round

Return all purchase cards to the center of the table.
The player who ended the purchase phase by not purchasing cards takes the starting player card, ⎈K.
If all players chose to purchase cards, the starting player card remains with the previous starting player.

Using the commodity deck, the starting player deals enough commodity cards to each player to bring their hand back to 6 cards, beginning with themselves and continuing clockwise around the table.
If needed, shuffle the cards of the discard pile and use them to refill the players' hands.

The next round begins as before with the starting player.


== End of the game

The game ends immediately when there are not enough cards in the commodity deck and the discard pile to bring all the players' hands back to 6 cards.

Each player counts their cash: each banknote is worth 1.
Cards in the players' hands, on their counter-tops and in their cellars are worth nothing.
The richest player wins.
If two or more players tie, the one among them with the fewest cards left in their cellar and on their counter-top together wins.
If there is still a tie, the tied players share the victory.
