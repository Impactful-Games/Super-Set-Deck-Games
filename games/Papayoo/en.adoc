= Papayoo
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 40 suited cards: 1-10 in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦ (the 1s are not used with 7 or 8 players).
* 20 Payoo cards: 1-20 in ★.
* 12 Papayoo determiner cards: the 3 **K**s of ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
* Paper and a pencil to keep score.
****

In Papayoo, avoid the tricks that hold the Payoo cards and, above all, the Papayoo: the player with the fewest points at the end of the game wins.


== Setup

Shuffle the suited cards and the Payoo (★) cards together to form the deck of game cards.

Place the K cards in a separate pile to form the deck of Papayoo determiners.


== Card ranking

Each Payoo (★) card is worth its own rank, from 1 to 20 points.
One of the four 7s of the four suits (♠, ♥, ♣ or ♦) is the Papayoo, which changes with each new round.
The Papayoo is worth 40 points.
The other cards have no value.


== Object of the game

Collect as few points as possible.


== Dealing the cards

Players take turns at dealing.
The dealer deals the game cards according to the table below.
For example, with 3 players, the dealer deals three cards to each player six times, and then two cards to each player once.
With 7 or 8 players, remove the 1s from the game before starting.

[%autowidth]
|===
| Players | Cards to remove before playing | Cards per player (deal) | Discard (see below)

>| 3 .4+^.^| none | *20* (6×3 + 1×2) | 5
>| 4 | *15* (5×3) | 5
>| 5 | *12* (4×3) | 4
>| 6 | *10* (3×3 + 1×2) | 3
>| 7 .2+^.^| the **1**s of ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦ | *8* (2×3 + 1×2) | 3
>| 8 | *7* (2×3 + 1×1) | 3
|===


=== Discarding

After sorting their cards, the players discard.
Each player chooses which cards to discard.
Each player removes the number of cards indicated in the table above from their hand, and places them face down in front of the player to their left.
Each player must give their discarded pile to the player on their left before looking at the discarded pile coming from the player on their right.


=== The Papayoo draw

Once the discarding is over, the dealer shuffles the Papayoo determiner cards (the K cards), draws one at random, and places it face up on top of the determiner pile to determine the Papayoo suit.
For example, if ♠K comes up, ♠7 is the Papayoo for this round and is worth 40 points.


== Playing a round

The dealer starts the first trick by placing any card face up in the middle of the table.
The other players then take turns playing, clockwise, always following the suit led.
Players do not have to play a card of higher rank.
The Payoo (★) cards are a fifth suit, just like ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
If a player does not have the suit led in their hand, they discard any card.
The player who played the highest card of the suit led collects the cards and starts the next trick.
If no player has cards of the suit led, the player who started collects the cards.


== End of a round

At the end of the last trick, the players count their points.
Each Payoo is worth its own rank.
The Papayoo is worth 40 points, and the other cards have no value.
The total score of a round should equal 250.
The players add these points to their previous score.


== End of the game

At the start of the game, the players decide how many rounds they wish to play.
Four rounds last about 30 minutes.
The player with the fewest points at the end of the last round is the winner.


== Remarks

When discarding, do not forget that discarding a complete suit to get the best discarding possibilities also means risking being left with a “lone” 7 and/or 9 and/or 10 from another player’s discarded pile!

To avoid winning a trick, players are often tempted to play lower cards than those played by the previous players.
However, you should know how to “take a few points” in the first tricks, when the stakes are low, to get rid of your highest cards and keep the lower ones for the following tricks.
This can even be an advantage for the last player of a trick, as they know exactly what the trick is worth.
