= Trump, Tricks, Game!
:toc: preamble
:toclevels: 4
:icons: font

[.ssd-components]
.Components
****
* 48 game cards: 1-12 in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
* 4 trump cards: A in ♠, ♥, ♣ & ♦.
* Paper and a pencil to keep the score.
****

In this game, you try to win tricks to get cards.
In the first three rounds, the number of tricks is predetermined.
In the fourth and last round, each player must win as many tricks as possible.

In addition to the basic game, there are two optional rules for a more challenging game.

The basic game is based on two mechanics:

* Tricks: the number of tricks a player may take is fixed in the first three rounds.
This way, each player receives the same number of tricks.
* Cards: in each following round, each player plays with the cards they won in tricks in the previous one.

Finally, the player with the most points wins.


== Setup

First, separate the game cards from the trump (A) cards.

* With 4 players, all the game cards are used.
* With 3 players, the game cards with a value of 1, 6 and 12 are not used.

Then shuffle the game cards.
Each player secretly receives 12 of them and takes them into their hand.

Now shuffle the trump (A) cards as well, and put them on the table.
Place each one slightly offset on top of the previous one, so that their order is visible.

The suits of the trump cards determine the trump suit of each round.
The top card gives the trump suit of the first round.

NOTE: The players know the trump suits of the next rounds.
Therefore, they may try to collect cards of these suits for them.

A sheet of paper and a pencil are handy to note the victory points of the players.


== General course of play

Trump, Tricks, Game! consists of 4 rounds.
The player who has the ♣10 starts the first round.
In the other rounds, the player with the most victory points starts.
A round consists of 12 tricks.

At the end of the first, second and third rounds, the players add up their points (see <<points>>).

IMPORTANT: In the first three rounds, each player may only take 3 tricks (with 4 players) or 4 tricks (with 3 players).

The cards won are the hands of the players for the next round: the cards are not reshuffled nor dealt again.
In the fourth round, each player tries to take as many tricks as possible.
Then it is important to catch as many cards as possible (whatever their values).


=== Playing a card

Play clockwise.
Each player always plays one card per trick.
One player wins the trick and leads the next one.
Cards with higher numbers beat cards with lower numbers.
Each player places the tricks they won face down next to them.


== Follow suit rule

In all rounds, the players must follow suit according to these rules:

* The suit of the first card played determines the "suit of the trick".
The other players must play cards of the same suit.
The player who plays the card with the highest number wins the trick.
* If a player does not have a card of this suit, they must play a card of another suit.
If they play a card of the current trump suit (look at the trump cards), it beats all the cards played earlier.
If there are several trump cards in the trick, the highest trump wins the trick.

.Trick
====
♠ is the trump suit.
Michael plays the ♥5.
Karen follows suit and plays the ♥7.
Daniel has no ♥ and trumps with the ♠8.
Peter has no ♥ card either, but does not want to take the trick.
Peter plays the ♣1.
Daniel wins the trick and leads the next one.
====


=== Predetermined number of tricks

When a player has already won the predetermined number of tricks, they cannot take any more tricks in this round.
The cards played by such a player no longer count.
However, this player must still play a card and follow suit.

You do *not* have to follow the suit of a card played by a player who has already won the predetermined number of tricks.
The first player who still needs tricks determines the "suit of the trick" with their card.

=== Catching cards

In the fourth and last round, the number of tricks is not predetermined.
The values of the cards no longer give points.
Now, it is only important to win as many tricks as possible.

Each player receives the following victory points for each card they won:

[%autowidth]
|===
h| Card suit        | ♠ | ♥ | ♣ | ♦
h| Number of points | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1
|===

These points are *added up*: they are not multiplied.


== Winning

The game ends after the fourth round.
Add up all the victory points (from all 4 rounds).
The player who scored the most points wins.


== Optional rules

Some players may find the basic game too dominated by luck.
Therefore, we have included the following two optional rules.
You can use them separately or together.


[#refusing-a-card]
=== Refusing a card

If you have only one card of the "suit of the trick" left in your hand, you may refuse it.
When you have to play a card, place it face up in front of you on the table: you may now play any other card (including a trump).

IMPORTANT: Each player may only have one refused card in front of them.
This card still counts as one of their hand cards, and must be played later according to the rules.
Once it has been played, another card may be refused.


=== Donating a trick

A player who wins a trick may keep it and put it in front of them (as in the basic game).
Alternatively, they may *donate* the trick to one of the other players who has not won the predetermined number of tricks yet.

They move the trick openly to the other player.
This player may take it, or try to refuse it.
To refuse it, they must have a hand card with a lower value than all the cards of the trick.
(So a trick with a 1 cannot be refused.
However, you may not donate a trick to the player who played its 1!)

IMPORTANT: The card used to refuse the trick must be of one of the suits of the cards of the trick.

.Refusing
====
To refuse a donated trick made of the ♥12, the ♥3, the ♠5 and the ♦6, a player must play a 1 or a 2 in ♥, ♠ or ♦.
====

If a donated trick is refused, the card used to refuse it is put into the trick, and the refusing player may take any card of the trick into their hand.

NOTE: If the refusing player takes a card of the same suit as the refused card lying in front of them (see <<refusing-a-card>>), they must put this refused card back into their hand.

If a donated trick is refused, the donor must take it: they may not try to donate it again.

The player who gets the trick leads the next one.
